# Design

Arclo is a month-end close workspace for finance teams. The page moves in one sequence: a compact navigation that contracts on scroll, a centered two-line headline over a textured ink-to-petrol field, a bordered close canvas, a quiet customer strip, three benefits, a three-plus-two feature grid, a central framework, three entity-based plans, four onboarding steps, finance stories, a split about section, journal, split FAQ and a closing panel that returns to the hero’s field.

## Palette

Ink, petrol and champagne. The hero and closing field run from near-black ink (#070d10) through deep petrol (#10303a, #1d5e6a) to a champagne light (#cdb98d) in the far corner, under a soft grain. Petrol (#287789) is the accent for links, active states and the chart; champagne appears only in small highlights, the glowing button’s edge and icon tints. White surfaces carry cool, barely-tinted greys so the page reads precise rather than warm. Icons use five quiet tints: petrol, champagne, ink, sage and slate.

## Type

Manrope is the only typeface. Desktop hero display is 52px at weight 800; main section headings are 44px at weight 750, cards 20px at weight 650, body 15–16px. Phone display is 36px and section headings 32px. Figures in the close canvas use tabular numerals so amounts line up.

## Product truth

The close canvas and the content checks share one dataset: three bank lines, three trial-balance accounts and three journal entries. Matching respects a tolerance you can change, variances are flagged only above 10% and $2,000, and approvals follow written limits. Every result is calculated from those rows, and every run can be saved as JSON. Nothing claims to post to a ledger or contact anyone. Stories describe qualitative outcomes without invented performance numbers.

## Motion

Native scrolling, small transform reveals, hover feedback and finite four-step runs. Decorative motion respects the system setting; nothing loops on its own, so there is no pause control. Reveals keep content visible from the first render. Phones use a compact menu, a readable two-by-two canvas, stacked features and plans, vertical onboarding steps, one story at a time and complete stacked forms.
