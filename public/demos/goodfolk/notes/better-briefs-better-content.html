Studio practice

The best content starts with a better brief.

By the studioOctober 2, 20262 minute read
WHAT IF?↗

A useful brief gives people a direction they can believe in. It makes the essential decisions early, so the creative work has room to move.

Name the actual job.

Are you introducing a new product, helping people see an existing one differently, or giving your community something to talk about? Choose the main job. A single piece of content can do several things, but asking it to do everything usually makes it less clear.

Describe a person, not a demographic.

Age and location are a starting point. The more useful questions are what someone cares about, when the product fits into their day, and what would make them pause. A specific human situation gives a creator much more to work with than a broad audience label.

Be precise about the essentials.

Write down the message that must land, the claims you can support, the production boundaries, and the usage rights. Put those decisions in the brief before the shoot. Clear constraints make creative freedom easier, because everyone knows where it begins and ends.

Agree what you want to learn.

Alongside the deliverables, write the question the campaign should help answer. It might be about a hook, a format, or a way of telling the story. A shared learning question makes the review more useful and gives the next round a clear place to start.
