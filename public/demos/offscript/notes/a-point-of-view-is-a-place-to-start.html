On strategy / 4 minute read

A point of view is a place to start.

The most useful creative brief is rarely the longest. It is the one that gives the work something to stand for.

From the Offscript studio notebook

Before the moodboard

It is tempting to start a new project by collecting things we like. A typeface, a photograph, a campaign from another category. References can help a conversation, but taste alone does not give the work a reason to exist.

A point of view starts earlier. What does the brand believe that its audience might care about? What does it see differently? What would feel wrong coming from anyone else? Those questions are harder to answer than choosing a color, which is exactly why they are useful.

Make a choice people can feel

A direction does not need to be loud to be distinctive. It might be a drink brand that celebrates doing nothing, a sports brand that makes room for play, or a lifestyle brand that gives personality more space than polish. The choice matters because it changes everything that follows.

Once there is a clear idea, the visual decisions have something to serve. The casting, the pace, the crop, the first line of copy and the final frame can all pull in the same direction. A campaign becomes a connected world rather than a collection of good-looking assets.

Leave enough room to make

A useful brief is specific about the problem and generous about the possibilities. It should describe the audience, the creative territory, the practical boundaries and what the work needs to do. It should leave the team enough room to surprise you.

We like to finish a brief with one sentence the whole team can remember. Not a slogan for the audience, but a compass for the work. If a decision supports that sentence, it belongs. If it does not, a beautiful reference is not enough to justify it.

The simplest test

Before production, ask whether the idea still makes sense without the moodboard. Can you explain why this brand would make this work, for these people, at this moment? If the answer is clear, the project has a place to start.

That clarity does not make the work predictable. It gives the unexpected parts a purpose. The best creative decisions often look surprising from the outside and completely natural from within the idea.
