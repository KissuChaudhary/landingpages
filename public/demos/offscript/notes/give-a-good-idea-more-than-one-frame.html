On making / 4 minute read

Give a good idea more than one frame.

A campaign can travel across formats without losing its character. The trick is to build a world, then make room inside it.

From the Offscript studio notebook

An idea, not a template

Consistency is easy to confuse with repetition. The same crop, the same headline and the same layout can make a feed look organized, but they can also flatten the life out of an idea. A creative system should hold the work together without making every piece identical.

We start with the elements that carry character. It might be the relationship between two colors, a particular kind of light, a voice with a small sense of humor, or a way of letting people fill the frame. Those elements can stay recognizable even when the format changes.

Let each format do its job

A wide campaign image, a vertical social frame and a short line of copy offer different kinds of attention. Treating them as versions of the same rectangle misses the opportunity. Each should express the idea in a way that feels natural to its setting.

That means planning for adaptation while making the original work. Leave useful negative space. Capture a detail and a wider view. Write a first line that can stand alone. Consider what a person might see before they decide to look closer.

Keep the handoff human

The finished assets are only part of the delivery. A short explanation of how the world works can be just as useful: what should stay consistent, what can change, and which decisions matter most. A team should be able to keep making without guessing what the original designer intended.

Clear names, usable crops and a few well-chosen examples help the work continue. A large folder of files is not the same as a good handoff. The aim is to give people the confidence to use the work, not to make them afraid of breaking it.

Leave the door open

A campaign becomes more interesting when there is room for a second chapter. The launch might introduce the world; a later piece can reveal a smaller detail, a different person or a fresh point of view. The system should allow that growth.

We ask a simple question at the end of a project: could someone make the next good piece with what we have given them? If the answer is yes, the idea has a life beyond the final frame.
