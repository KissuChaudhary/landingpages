Run clubs aren't a trend. They're a habit.

Why a weekly community program can outperform a launch, and what it takes to keep one running.

Sam KellerCommunity Lead

Brands have discovered run clubs, and plenty of them will fade by next summer. The ones that last aren't really about running. They're about a reason to see the same people every week, and brands that understand that end up with the most loyal channel they have.

Frequency beats scale

A launch party reaches a few hundred people once. A weekly club reaches fewer people, but it reaches them fifty times a year. By the third month, members are bringing friends, posting without being asked and asking what's next.

That's why we measure community programs by return rate, not attendance. If more than half the group comes back the following week, the program is working.

Hosts make or break it

The single biggest factor is the host. We recruit hosts from the community itself, people who already run, cook or gather, and we train and pay them. A good host remembers names, notices newcomers and makes the slowest runner feel like the reason everyone showed up.

People join for the activity and stay for each other. Your job is to make the second part easy.

Rituals over swag

Free merchandise brings people once. Rituals bring them back: the same start time, the same finish point, a photo at the end, a small token for first-timers that regulars recognise. Northpaw's bandanas cost almost nothing and became the club's badge.

Hand it over

We design every program to run without us. After the first season we hand over the routes, hosts, member list and a content kit, so the brand owns the community it built, which is the whole point.
