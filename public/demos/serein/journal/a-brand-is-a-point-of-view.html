Identity / August 2026

A brand is a point of view.

Before the wordmark, the palette or the website, there should be something worth saying.

A visual identity can tell you what a business looks like. A point of view tells you why it deserves to exist. The two work best when they are built together.

At the start of a project, we spend time looking for the useful difference. Sometimes it is an unfamiliar way of solving a familiar problem. Sometimes it is the founder's instinct about what a category has forgotten. It usually shows up in a conversation, before it shows up in a brief.

That difference needs to be specific enough to guide a decision. 'We care about quality' is difficult to design from. 'We make listening feel less like another task' opens up a world of choices: the language, the materials, the pace of a website.

Once the idea is clear, consistency becomes more than repeating a logo. Photography, product language, packaging and interactions can express the same belief in different ways. The brand begins to feel coherent without looking identical everywhere.

A useful identity system also leaves room for the business to grow. It gives a team principles they can use when the designer is no longer in the room. What belongs? What feels right? What would we never say?

The strongest brands are recognizable before you notice the mark. You feel their point of view in the choices they make. Our job is to help make that point of view clear, and give it the tools to travel.
