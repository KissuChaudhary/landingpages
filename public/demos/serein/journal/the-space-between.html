The space between.

Why the moments between things matter as much as the things themselves.

We tend to talk about websites as collections of sections. A hero, a portfolio, a contact form. But the experience is also made of everything that connects them: the space, the rhythm, the change in tone.

Architecture makes this easy to understand. A doorway can prepare you for the room beyond it. A narrow passage can make a courtyard feel expansive. The transition changes how you experience the destination.

A website has similar opportunities. A change from dark to light can signal a new chapter. An image that holds in place for a moment can give you time to notice its details. A small response to a pointer can make the next action feel clear.

The danger is treating those moments as a performance. Movement that competes with reading becomes a tax on attention. A beautiful transition that leaves a visitor waiting has forgotten its purpose.

We look for motion that follows the content. It should be quick enough to respect someone's time, and deliberate enough to establish a rhythm. On a smaller screen, or for someone who prefers less movement, that same relationship should remain clear.

The space between is where an experience becomes comfortable. You might not remember a particular transition. You remember that finding your way felt natural. That is a detail worth getting right.
