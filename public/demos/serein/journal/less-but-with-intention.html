Perspective / September 2026

Less. But with intention.

Restraint isn't the absence of an idea. It's knowing which one matters.

There is a point in every project when adding something is easier than making a decision. Another feature. Another color. Another line of explanation. The work grows, but the idea gets harder to see.

Restraint asks a different question: what is this here to do? A headline can set a tone. An image can make a material feel real. A transition can explain a relationship. When the purpose is clear, the design has less to prove.

That doesn't mean everything should be minimal. Some brands need energy, abundance and a little friction. The point is to make those choices deliberately. A crowded page can be considered. An empty page can be careless.

We often begin a review by taking something away. Not to make the work quieter, but to understand which part is doing the real work. If the idea survives, the extra detail might have been covering it rather than supporting it.

The same discipline applies to motion. A small change in scale can tell you that an image is a doorway. A paced reveal can help you read a sequence. Movement earns its place when it gives something back to the person looking.

A good final detail doesn't call attention to the effort behind it. It makes the whole experience feel inevitable. That is the kind of restraint we are interested in: less to navigate, more to remember.
